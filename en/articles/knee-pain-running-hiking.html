Pain at the front of the knee after running or hiking: patellofemoral pain and three simple exercises

Does the front of your knee ache on the way down after a hike, or after a run? This article explains a common cause and introduces three exercises you can do at home.

In shortPain at the front of the knee after running or hiking is often patellofemoral pain (pain around the kneecap). It typically worsens when going downstairs, squatting, sitting for long periods or running. Guidelines recommend exercise as the main treatment, training both the hip and knee muscles, while temporarily adjusting the activities that aggravate the pain. If the knee is clearly swollen, locks, or cannot bear weight after an injury, please see a doctor or physiotherapist.

What is patellofemoral pain?

Patellofemoral pain is pain behind or around the kneecap, and one of the most common types of knee pain among runners and hikers.

The pain usually appears when the knee takes more load: squatting, climbing or descending stairs, walking downhill, jumping, running, or after sitting for a long time. Symptoms can fluctuate and may recur.

You may not need to stop exercising; the key is adjusting how much you do.

Guidelines recommend exercise as the main treatment, with a temporary reduction in activities that clearly worsen the pain, such as downhill walking, stairs or long runs, followed by a gradual return.

Three exercises you can do at home

Guidelines recommend training both the hip and knee muscles, not the knee alone. The numbers below are a starting point only; adjust them to suit you.

Points to note

  • Aim for no pain or only mild discomfort. If the pain clearly worsens, or is worse the next day, reduce the range or the number of repetitions.
  • Move slowly and keep your knee in line with your toes.
  • When returning to running or hiking, start with shorter distances and build up gradually.

When to seek medical advice first

  • The knee is clearly swollen, warm or red
  • The knee locks, or often gives way
  • After a fall or twist, you cannot walk normally or bear weight
  • The pain has not improved after several weeks

Common questions

Can I keep running or hiking with knee pain?

Often yes, but adjust how much you do, for example by shortening distances and reducing downhill sections, so that the pain does not clearly worsen. If in doubt, please consult a physiotherapist.

Why train the hips for knee pain?

The hip muscles help control the position of the thigh and knee when running or going downstairs. Guidelines recommend training both the hip and knee, not the knee alone.

Do I need an X-ray or MRI?

Patellofemoral pain is mainly assessed through your history and an examination, so imaging is not always needed. If another problem is suspected, a doctor will arrange what is required.
