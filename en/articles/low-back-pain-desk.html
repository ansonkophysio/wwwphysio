Low back pain from sitting all day? Why it happens and three simple moves

Sat all day and your back feels stiff and sore when you stand up? This article explains the back pain office workers often get, and three simple moves.

Short answerMost low back pain has no single serious cause; guidelines call it "non-specific low back pain", and most of it gradually improves. Guidelines recommend keeping up your daily activities and exercise and avoiding long bed rest, and most people don't need an X-ray or MRI straight away. If you lose control of your bladder or bowels, have numbness around the groin, or weakness in both legs, get medical help straight away.

Why does my back hurt?

Most low back pain can't be traced to a single, clear structural problem. Guidelines call this "non-specific low back pain".

Staying in one sitting position for a long time can leave your back feeling stiff and tired.

With back pain, moving a bit usually beats resting in bed.

Research comparing advice to rest in bed with advice to stay active found that people with acute low back pain who stayed active had slightly better pain and function.

Guidelines also say most back pain doesn't need an X-ray or MRI straight away; a doctor will arrange one if something serious is suspected.

Three simple moves

Choose moves that feel comfortable and that you can keep doing, without making the pain clearly worse. The numbers below are a starting point.

While you do them

  • Move slowly, within a comfortable range.
  • If the pain clearly gets worse while you do a move, stop.
  • Keep up your daily activities as much as you can, building up gradually.

When should I get help straight away?

  • Loss of bladder or bowel control, or difficulty passing urine
  • Numbness around the groin or inner thighs
  • Weakness in both legs, or numbness that keeps getting worse
  • Fever, or back pain after a serious injury such as a fall from height
  • Weight loss you can't explain

If it hasn't improved after four weeks, or the back pain comes with leg pain or numbness, please also see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Common questions

Should I rest in bed with back pain?

Long bed rest generally isn't recommended. Research found that people with acute low back pain who stayed active had slightly better pain and function. Adjust your activity to your pain.

Do I need an X-ray or MRI for back pain?

Usually not straight away. Guidelines recommend scans only when something serious is suspected, and a doctor decides based on your situation.

Which exercise is best for back pain?

Guidelines recommend exercise therapy for ongoing back pain. The right exercise varies from person to person, and a physiotherapist can suggest what suits you.
